# CANOPY | Unified conceptual masterplan

Status: **reference model, pre-feasibility**. No real site has been selected or surveyed.

## Mission and design rules

CANOPY integrates six compact, walkable urban districts with one continuous watershed and habitat network. Environmental carrying capacity, people and land tenure precede architecture. Measured biodiversity, flood safety, housing access, utility reliability, public health and lifecycle affordability matter more than decorative greenery.

## Reference scenario

| Parameter | Illustrative assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Study envelope | 12 km², approximately 4 × 3 km |
| Maturity population | 60,000 people |
| Districts | Six, roughly 10,000 people per district |
| Built form | 4–7-story mixed-use blocks; selected transit sites may be studied separately |
| Urban organization | River / riparian reserve, upland habitat, six linked centers and food landscapes |
| Energy and water | Five interconnected but safely separable systems |

### Spatial allocation test

| Category | Share | Area |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Building footprints | 15% | 1.80 km² |
| Habitat, water, protection and restoration | 48% | 5.76 km² |
| Productive and communal open land | 25% | 3.00 km² |
| Mobility, hardscape and essential utilities | 12% | 1.44 km² |

These are **different surface categories**, not additive promises that 85% of land is wild. Building footprints are not the same as entire neighborhood boundaries. Surveyed habitat and hazard exclusions can force revisions or rejection of a site.

### The six neighborhoods

| District | Primary function | Spatial character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| West Grove | Homes, schools, neighborhood food | Preserve mature canopy and maintain gentle edges |
| Ridge Commons | Civic learning, libraries, labs, clinics | Upland transit interchange and public terraces |
| East Terrace | Mixed homes, jobs and cultural activity | Shaded stepping, regional access |
| Meadow Works | Repair, circular manufacturing and training | Buffered low-impact service edge |
| Garden Forum | Growing, markets, processing and education | Agroforestry and experimental cultivation |
| Riverlight | Research, elevated housing, wetland stewardship | Boardwalk access outside protected habitats |

The river is a **protected waterway**, not a theme-park canal. Bridges must minimize fragmentation, flooding and wildlife disturbance. Paths may close during nesting or restoration periods. The baseline concept avoids construction in active floodplains.

## Architectural prototype

The Living Courtyard is a code-reviewed timber or timber-hybrid 4–7-story block with a planted shared court (illustrative section: 30 m), passive solar shading, cross ventilation where feasible, accessible circulation, robust fire/seismic design, and physically separated rooftop solar and ecological planting. Biofacades, algae systems and kinetic elements belong to monitored replaceable pilot bays.

## Streets and movement

An illustrative 24 m green transit avenue includes walking and accessibility, protected bike lanes, planting buffers, an electric tram or BRT corridor, and essential service/emergency access. Local transport is walking, cycling and accessible micromobility first; trams connect districts; on-demand electric shuttles close accessibility gaps. Subsurface freight and aerial pods are optional feasibility studies, not requirements.

## Five resource systems

1. **Energy and heat**: passive envelopes; solar, carefully evaluated wind, thermal loops, storage, flexible demand, and grid interconnection. Resilience design addresses islanding and critical-load tiers, not unlimited autonomy.
2. **Water**: lawful intake, conservation, rain capture, separated grey/black water, treatment, verified reuse, and clean discharge. Constructed wetlands supplement, but do not replace, required treatment and disinfection.
3. **Food**: gardens, orchards, agroforestry, rooftop growing, local food hubs and regenerative regional supply. Fresh produce has a different feasibility profile from calorie independence.
4. **Materials**: reversible assemblies, certified timber hybrids, approved low-carbon materials, component passports, repair shops and local circular manufacturing.
5. **Information**: local-first sensors and an auditable watershed digital twin; ecological data open at safe resolutions; private human data minimized. Certified physical controls override algorithms.

## Planning calculations, not performance claims

- Annual gross electrical demand scenario: 60,000 × 4,000 kWh = **240 GWh**.
- Solar example: 105 MWp × 8,760 h × 19% assumed capacity factor ≈ **174.8 GWh/year**.
- Wind example: 25 MW × 8,760 h × 30% assumed capacity factor ≈ **65.7 GWh/year**.
- Combined annual generation example: **240.5 GWh** before temporal matching, losses, habitat constraints and curtailment. Annual sums alone do not establish islanding or net-zero operation.
- Daily gross water scenario: 60,000 × 110 L = 6.6 ML residential, plus 1.4 ML shared = **8 ML/day**.
- Reclaimed non-potable water planning target: **2–3 ML/day**, subject to treatment, regulations and fit-for-purpose uses.
- Critical-load emergency example: 5 MW × 72 hours = **360 MWh** delivered before losses. Full-city two-week autonomy is not an adopted claim.

## Decision safeguards

Human permits, democratic review, ecology expertise, water rights, Indigenous and existing community participation, independent safety review, privacy, housing affordability, fire access, accessibility and cost controls are required. A projected ecological harm triggers study, mitigation, redesign or a stop decision, not automatic displacement or unilateral model control.

## Variants

The baseline visual is a temperate upland river valley. A tidal delta variant adds saltwater, erosion, retreat and marsh concerns. A hot dryland variant adds strict water-rights, shade, fire and dry-cooling constraints. The same rendering must not be mistaken for three feasible sites.
